Wembley Awaits as Whistle FC Prepare for Vertu Trophy Final

📅 10 April 2026 | Match Preview

Match Information

Fixture: Whistle FC vs Stockport County

Competition: Vertu Trophy Final

Date: Sunday 12 April 2026

Kick-off: 3:00pm

Venue: Wembley Stadium

Whistle FC will head to Wembley Stadium this Sunday as Johannes Hoff Thorup’s side prepare for one of the biggest matches in the club’s modern history — the Vertu Trophy Final against Stockport County.

After a campaign packed with growing belief, the Whistlers now stand just one game away from silverware on the biggest domestic stage in English football.

With thousands of supporters expected to make the journey to London, Wembley is set to be awash with red and black as the club prepares for a landmark occasion in its modern rise.

The final represents another major milestone for Whistle Football Club, who continue to build momentum both on and off the pitch under Head Coach Johannes Hoff Thorup.

Strong Form at the Perfect Time

Whistle come into Sunday’s final in excellent form and full of confidence, having put together a strong run of performances in recent weeks.

Victories over Wycombe Wanderers, Exeter City, Peterborough United and most recently AFC Wimbledon have helped strengthen belief within the squad, while the team’s consistency has underlined the progress made throughout the season.

Recent Form

  • 6 Apr 2026: AFC Wimbledon 0–3 Whistle FC
  • 3 Apr 2026: Whistle FC 2–1 Peterborough United
  • 21 Mar 2026: Whistle FC 1–1 Stockport County
  • 17 Mar 2026: Whistle FC 3–2 Exeter City
  • 14 Mar 2026: Wycombe Wanderers 1–2 Whistle FC
  • 10 Mar 2026: Doncaster Rovers 1–1 Whistle FC

Momentum has continued to grow at just the right time, with Whistle showing both attacking quality and the resilience required to handle the pressure of big occasions.

Thorup’s Message

Speaking ahead of the final, Johannes Hoff Thorup emphasised the importance of embracing the occasion while staying fully focused on the task ahead:

“These are the matches everyone wants to be involved in. Wembley is a special place, but our focus has to stay on the performance. We’ve earned the right to be here and now we want to finish the job.”

Thorup also highlighted the mentality his side must take into the game, insisting that finals are won through discipline, courage and trust in the team’s identity.

“You cannot play a final only with emotion. You need emotion, of course, because it means a lot to everybody, but you also need clarity. We have to be brave, we have to be disciplined and we have to trust the way we play.”

On the Occasion

The Whistle head coach admitted the scale of the day will not be lost on his players, but believes the squad has earned the right to enjoy the moment.

“You should enjoy days like this because they are special. Football is about moments, and this is a big moment for our club, our players and our supporters. But when the whistle goes, it is still football, and we must be ready to compete.”

Thorup was also quick to praise the supporters who have followed the team throughout the campaign and are now preparing to back the side at Wembley.

“The supporters have been incredible. They’ve followed the team everywhere and they’ve pushed us in difficult moments. To see them at Wembley will be special for the players, and we want to give them a performance they can be proud of.”

Belief in the Squad

Asked about the mood within the dressing room, Thorup spoke confidently about the belief and togetherness that has developed within the group over recent months.

“I believe a lot in this group. They’ve shown character, they’ve shown togetherness and they’ve shown that they can handle pressure. What matters now is that we bring our level onto the pitch on Sunday.”

The Whistle boss also stressed that reaching the final should not be viewed as the end of the journey, but as an opportunity to take another major step forward as a club.

“To get to Wembley is an achievement, yes, but we don’t just want to arrive there. We want to go there with ambition. We want to show who we are, and we want to try to bring the trophy back with us.”

The Opposition

Standing in Whistle’s way are a strong Stockport County side, who have also enjoyed an impressive campaign in League One and will arrive at Wembley with plenty of confidence of their own.

Stockport’s recent run includes victories over Wycombe Wanderers, AFC Wimbledon and Northampton Town, as well as a draw away at Bolton Wanderers, highlighting the challenge they will pose on the national stage.

Stockport County Recent Form

  • 28 Mar 2026: Stockport County 3–0 AFC Wimbledon

  • 17 Mar 2026: Stockport County 2–1 Northampton Town

Thorup expects a demanding test and believes Whistle will need to be at their best to come out on top.

“Stockport are a very good side. They are organised, they are aggressive and they have quality in important areas. We know it will be a tough game, and that is exactly what you expect in a final.”

Head-to-Head This Season

The two sides are no strangers to one another, having already met twice in EFL League One this season.

2025/26 Meetings

  • 8 Nov 2025: Stockport County 0–3 Whistle FC
  • 21 Mar 2026: Whistle FC 1–1 Stockport County

Whistle’s dominant away victory at Edgeley Park remains one of the standout results of the season, while the more recent draw at the Mornflake Stadium showed just how evenly matched Sunday’s final could be.

A Huge Occasion for the Club

For Whistle supporters, this is about far more than just one football match. It is another defining moment in the story of a club that has fought its way back through the football pyramid and now stands on the brink of major silverware.

The journey from non-league football back to the national stage has been built on unity, resilience and ambition — values that continue to define the club today.

Everyone at Whistle Football Club is encouraging supporters to make Wembley a sea of red and black and bring the noise from first whistle to last.

Wembley. Sunday. One more push.
